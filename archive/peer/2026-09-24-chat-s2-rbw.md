## Answer

**I couldn't refute the claim. It holds, and the removed wires account for exactly the 22 missing edges.** Most of my points narrow it rather than overturn it. One correction to the framing: the fix belongs in a different place than `op_remove_bad_wires`.

**1. Strongest reason the claim could have been wrong, and what the files show.** The explanation rested on a count ("13 wires qualify before any action") and seven spot-checked wires. The log prints only 8 of the 22 missing edges (`sim_l7_split.py:112` slices them to `[:8]`). Neither showed that the 22 missing edges are exactly the edges of the wires the simulator removed. I checked this against the recorded step states:
- Step 45 removed 16 wires: `[1581, 3268, 3629, 4517, 13486, 16880, 19372, 23614, 25186, 25404, 25478, 26025, 28924, 31169, 31193, 34066]` (from `sim/l7_split/step_45_remove_bad_wires.json`).
- In `step_44_delete_object.json`:
  - Wires 1581, 3629 and 4517 have one source and no sink. They are #376's real half-wires, so removing them costs no edge.
  - The other 12 wires each have one Diagram-owned source listed twice, with 1, 1, 2, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 2, 1, 1 and 2 sinks. That is 15 edges.
  - **Wire 3268** also has a duplicated source (term 644 on #639) and 7 live sinks besides #376 (terminals 29256, 12200, 3509, 10083, 3272, 3211, 2218). That is 7 edges.
- 15 + 7 = **22**. This matches the diff exactly, which rules out a second edge-removing action.
- It also explains "13 before any action": the 13th wire is 3268. It is #376's own branch wire, which is why the "ruled out" list did not flag it.

**2. An alternative I checked and rejected: the duplicate comes from stagesim, not the reader.** It is already in the recorded input. `graph_s3_loop15_20260924.json` holds term 13410 twice. `base_state` copies it unchanged (`stagesim.py:170`). The code already knew about the duplicate: `resolve_addr` dedupes on `(term_uid, term_class, frame_diagram)` at `stagesim.py:255`, but `op_remove_bad_wires` does not (`stagesim.py:572`).

**3. Did LabVIEW's real Remove Bad Wires delete these wires? The recorded evidence says no.**
- In the real L7-R run, RBW removed 7 wires (1920 → 1913). The uid edges read after RBW were identical to those read before it (`stage_d1_l7_r_r2.log:446-448`).
- The real reader also builds edges from the duplicated sources: `vigraph.py:294` loops over every source row. So these 22 edges were in the live graph read after RBW.
- NI documents the method only as removing "broken wires" ([NI: Block Diagram:Remove Bad Wires](https://www.ni.com/docs/en-US/bundle/labview/page/lvscript/vi_block_diagram058remove_bad_wires.html), [LabVIEW Wiki](https://labviewwiki.org/wiki/VI_class/Block_Diagram.Remove_Bad_Wires_method)). Wires with no connection at either end are a separate method ([Remove Wire Loose Ends](https://labviewwiki.org/wiki/AbstractDiagram_class/Remove_Wire_Loose_Ends_method)). Nothing I found says a wire is broken because a reader lists its source twice.

**Where I would correct the fix.**
- **Counting distinct `term_uid` is necessary, but RBW is the wrong place for it.** `vigraph.build4` flags wires on the same raw row count (`vigraph.py:290-292`). So the base graph `G3` already carries 13 false flags. Gate C4 only passes because it compares *new* flags (`sim_l7_split.py:93-95`). Any other consumer of `flags` inherits the error. Dedupe the rows once, where they are loaded (in `base_state`, or better in the reader), not only in `op_remove_bad_wires`.
- **Do not take LabVIEW's count of 7 as the target.** After the fix the simulator will remove 3 wires (1581, 3629, 4517), not 7. The other 4 are probably loose wire pieces with no terminal rows, or wires broken by data type. The terminal graph cannot see either kind. That is a known limit of the model, not a defect to chase.
- **An open reader question, not needed for this gate.** Every duplicated source is Diagram-owned, unnamed and of class `Terminal`, possibly an iteration or selector terminal inside a structure. That guess is not verified. Why the reader emits it twice deserves one line in the reader's known-defects list.

**What would falsify the claim.**
- After deduping, the replay still shows a nonzero diff.
- Or a duplicated `term_uid` appears on one wire as both source and sink. Then counting distinct uids would hide a genuinely broken wire.

**Cheapest discriminating test (pure Python, about 46 s, no LabVIEW):**
1. Over `graph_s3_loop15_20260924.json`, list every `(wire_uid, term_uid)` that appears more than once. Expect all of them to be Diagram-owned sources with a consistent `is_source`, and the set of their wires to equal the 13 above.
2. Dedupe on `term_uid` inside `op_remove_bad_wires` (or at load) and rerun `sim_l7_split.py`. Expect:
   - L7-R canon diff = 0,
   - `removed_wires == [1581, 3629, 4517]`,
   - C6 still showing the one row `(376, 'current frame data array in')`.

   Any other result reopens the question.
