## Answer

**The conclusion may well be right, but "E1 = 1" is not evidence for it.** At E1 both case frames were still empty, so it only shows that the selector wiring compiles. Everything that makes up the gate's real end state appears only after the moves:
- the control terminal #8476 sitting inside a conditional frame,
- the indicator #8323 inside B,
- the default-valued output tunnel #23927 feeding the loop tunnel #11363.

E3 = 0 therefore cannot separate "a move left debris" from "the finished gate is itself illegal." The claim needs a positive sign of debris, and the run already recorded one.

## 1. Why "E1 = 1" doesn't clear the design, and why reading E2 wouldn't have helped
- The second value `connect_from_wire` returns is `exec_state` (`tools/recipes/build_opconnectfromwire_v0.py:420`). Every re-wiring step in both moves reads 0 (`fgate_97_stage2.log:314-378`), including A's last step (`:354`).
- That was always going to happen: A's output tunnel has no value in the False frame until `tunnel_use_default` runs, and that only happens after move B (`:388`).
- So an E2 read between the moves would also have been 0. Leaving E2 out cost nothing, but the missing E2 is not a gap you can close by reading it. The step order hides every intermediate ExecState.
- Side note: the `(0, 0, …)` results at `:334` and `:345` are not failures. The first value is the change in wire count, and a branch adds no Wire object (`tools/gscript.py:2058-2061`).

## 2. The edge-table check cannot see this kind of fault
- `_c97_pairs` builds (source × sink) pairs from wires that have both ends (`tools/gscript.py:3470-3475`).
- A wire with no terminals, only a source, or only sinks produces no pair.
- The after-table is also filtered to member terminals (`:3638`).
- So "16 = 16 edges" is fully compatible with orphaned or dangling wires, which LabVIEW lists as "broken wire with loose ends" and which make the VI not runnable ([LabVIEW Core 1 troubleshooting](https://quizlet.com/278831713/labview-core-1-chapter-3-troubleshooting-flash-cards/)). LabVIEW has a scripting method just for these: [Remove Wire Loose Ends](https://labviewwiki.org/wiki/AbstractDiagram_class/Remove_Wire_Loose_Ends_method).

## 3. Evidence the run already holds: more Wire objects than the edges explain
This rests on my own counting model, not a measurement. Under it, a wire that crosses into a frame becomes outer + inner (+1), and an internal edge that is re-made should be one-for-one (0).

| | wires crossing the frame | expected change | observed change | surplus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Move A | 4 (8643, w31064, w31128, w11374) | +4 | 1903 → 1915 = **+12** (`fgate_97_stage.json:407,420`) | ~8 |
| Move B | 2 (11614, 11369) | +2 | 1915 → 1918 = **+3** (`fgate_97_stage.json:803,816`) | 1 |

- B has exactly one internal edge (11267 → #8323) and a surplus of exactly 1.
- A has 10 internal edges and a surplus of 8. Fan-out among the old wires would bring 10 down toward 8.
- Best-fitting reading: when both ends of an internal edge move, `move_into_frame` re-makes the edge but leaves the old severed wire behind as an orphan.
- Consequence: your two competing explanations are probably not either/or. **Both moves likely contribute** (A about 8 orphans, B about 1).

## 4. Where your two alternatives stand
- **(1) One of 9908/23402/23817 is an unassigned output tunnel:** weak.
  - Four new tunnels match 3 source wires in + 1 out.
  - The collapse only joins a sink to a source on the same tunnel, and `missing=[]`, so every edge passes through a tunnel that has a source.
  - The low uids are LabVIEW reusing numbers: #9259 was minted again and again (`log:143,192,214`).
- **(2) Move B is the break:** fits, but through the same orphan mechanism. An indicator terminal inside a case frame is legal.

## 5. What would falsify the claim
On a replayed scratch copy:
- no orphan or dangling wires after the moves,
- and the wire-count surplus fully explained by fan-out,
- and ExecState still 0 after `tunnel_use_default`.

Then the break is in the gate's own post-move meaning after all.

## 6. Cheapest test that separates the claim from the alternative
1. **No LabVIEW at all.** From the ops list in `fgate_97_stage.json` and `edges_before`, count the distinct old wires per move (fan-out included) and compute the exact expected wire change. If the surplus survives, the orphan hypothesis stands before the next run.
2. **Next run, read-only, fresh scratch.**
   - Move `tunnel_use_default` to **straight after move A**, then read ExecState (E2′). Only this order makes E2 informative.
   - After each move, compare `uids(target, "Wire")` against the wire uids in `allterms.read_terms`. Flag three things: wires that appear in no row, wires with only a source, and wires with only sinks. List each one's owning diagram. This names the break by uid and says which move left it, without a VI run or the GUI.
3. **Confirmation (changes the scratch, scratch only).** Run `remove_bad_wires_scripted` (`gscript.py:2779`; NI docs: [Remove Bad Wires](https://www.ni.com/docs/en-US/bundle/labview/page/lvscript/vi_block_diagram058remove_bad_wires.html)).
   - If the wire count drops by about 9, ExecState goes to 1, and the collapsed edge table is unchanged: orphans confirmed. The fix then belongs inside `move_into_frame`: delete severed old wires, or run loose-end removal on the frame's diagram.
   - If Remove Bad Wires also deletes a **member** edge: that points to a type or tunnel break instead, because it removes type-mismatch wires too (`tools/bench/bool_wire_probe.py:5`).

## Bottom line
"The moves broke it" is probably correct, but the reason is the wire-count surplus plus the edge table's blind spot, not E1. What would change my mind is step 2 above coming back with no orphans while ExecState stays 0. I have not measured anything: the surplus is arithmetic from recorded counts under my own counting model.
