## 2. The claim's LabVIEW premise is also false on its own terms

**A Local variable that READS an indicator with no writer is legal LabVIEW, not a broken-VI condition.** NI's own list of what breaks a VI is short: a broken wire (type mismatch or loose end), an unwired **required** block-diagram terminal, or a broken/re-connector-paned subVI ([NI — Correcting Broken VIs](https://www.ni.com/docs/en-US/bundle/labview/page/correcting-broken-vis.html), [NI — Debugging Techniques](https://knowledge.ni.com/KnowledgeArticleDetails?id=kA00Z000001DbgJSAS)). A sourceless indicator is none of them — an unwired front-panel terminal is a **warning**, and warnings do not break a VI. The LabVIEW Wiki states the direction rule plainly: locals "read or write to one of the Controls or Indicators on the Front Panel", and reading an indicator is explicitly allowed; "One Write, Multiple Reads" is offered as a **style** rule, not a legality rule ([LabVIEW Wiki — Local Variable](https://labviewwiki.org/wiki/Local_Variable)).

And the run contains its own counter-example, as the previous review already established and the plan already accepted (`archive/peer/2026-09-21-c62-localplacement.md:111-117`, disposed at `docs/cycle27-plan.md:2484-2487`): the bed opens at `ExecState` **1** (`:19`) carrying eleven bare terminals on `#637` alone (`:24-25`, all `'wire': 0, 'errs': [0,0,0,1055]`), indices 43/44 being both sides of one completely dead tunnel.
