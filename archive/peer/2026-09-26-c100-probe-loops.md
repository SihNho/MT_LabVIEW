## Answer

I checked the claim against the graph file. It holds: the three failures come from the script's own gate, not from missing data. Two points are weaker than you state them.

**Measured in `par1359_95_graph.json`:**
- **All three loops are in `objs` with the expected class.** `par1359_95_graph.json:80357-80358` is uid 637 `"class": "WhileLoop"`, `:73850-73851` is uid 25380 `"WhileLoop"`, and `:93965-93966` is uid 1359 `"ForLoop"`. So the loops are not missing from this file.
- **The file is S1.** Log line 4 shows the md5 gate passing (3e3d23ce), and log line 6 shows `vi` = `claudeDev\D1_s1_copy.vi`.
- **No loop owns any terminal row.** A search for `"owner_class": "WhileLoop|ForLoop|TimedLoop"` finds 0 matches in all 5,748 rows. The loop's own `i` and stop terminals are listed under the body Diagram. For example, `#639 Diagram t644 src=True w3268` is the `i` feeding `1114 'index i'` (log lines 13 and 204), and `t648 src=False w3457` is the stop, fed by the `TurnOff` wire (log line 222).
- **The loop ↔ body links match in a second file, but that file is a different VI.** `sim/l2a1/graph_k_80_owners.json` has `"639":["WhileLoop",637]`, `"25392":["WhileLoop",25380]` and `"7911":["ForLoop",1359]`. It is D1_k (md5 6cf5…), not S1, so the link is inferred from uids carrying over between copies. It is not measured on S1.

**1. Strongest reason the claim is wrong: your fix may be wrong, even though your diagnosis is right.** The table has no loop-to-body link. `objs[].owner` holds only a class name (log line 8: `"owner": "TopLevelDiagram"`), not a uid. So "637's body is 639" cannot be read from `par1359_95_graph.json` at all. Changing the gate to "uid in objs" only proves the loop exists. It does not show that the rows R1–R8 attach to the right loop. That is exactly the check that matters.

**2. Alternative explanation: the table is lossy for loop terminals, not just organised a different way.**
- The ForLoop body #7911 shows `t7921` twice and no N (count) terminal (log lines 85-86).
- The same duplication appears on 639 (`t644`×2, `t648`×2) and on 25392 (`t25406`×2, `t25410`×2).
- The `flags` in `graph_s1` record wire 3268 with `n_src 2` (log line 268), which is one terminal seen as two sources.

So "a loop's terminals are under its Diagram" may partly be an artefact of how the extractor merged the rows. Any row that has to wire a loop's `N`, `i` or stop terminal cannot trust this table. In VI Scripting a loop reaches its `i` and condition terminals through its own properties ("LoopCounter" and "LoopEndRef" in the forum thread below), not through its body diagram's terminal list. That is second-hand from a forum answer. I did not check it against NI's own documentation.

**3. What would falsify the claim:**
- A rows_spec row that needs a terminal *owned by* 637, 25380 or 1359, such as N or `i` wired from outside the table. In that case the failure is a real hole in the data, not a script bug.
- Or: the tunnels' inner faces not landing on 639, 25392 or 7911 in S1.

**4. Cheapest test that separates the two (extend probe2):**
- For each tunnel/shift-register row in S1 (for example `t9230 fd7911 InnerTerminal`), check that the inner `frame_diagram` is the body (639, 25392, 7911).
- Check that the outer `frame_diagram` equals `graph_k.owners[<loop>][1]`: 686 for 637 and 25380, 639 for 1359.
- Grep S1's `terminals` for any row whose `frame_diagram == 7911` and whose name or class looks like a count (N) terminal.
- Grep `rows_spec_100.md` for any row that addresses a terminal of 637, 25380 or 1359 directly.

If the tunnel faces match and no row needs a loop terminal, the claim stands and the "uid in objs" gate is enough. What would change my mind: any rows_spec row that depends on N, `i` or stop, or a tunnel face that does not match.
