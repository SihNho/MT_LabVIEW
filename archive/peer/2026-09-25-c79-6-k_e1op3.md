## Answer

**Verdict: the claim is wrong.** Its topology and its mechanism are both refuted by the files already on disk. One part survives: the fault really is in the simulator, not in LabVIEW or the recipe, and E1 should not be relaxed. But the fix the claim proposes, "model whole-wire loss on move", is the wrong fix.

## 1. Strongest reason it is wrong: there is no single branched wire, and #5058 is upstream of #2765, not downstream

In `step_02_move_in.json`, the simulator's graph just before op 3:

- **#5058 is the source.** Its output `'x,y,z array out'` (terminal 5124, `is_source:true`) drives **wire 505**. Wire 505 ends at three sinks: tunnel #2765's outside terminal (2811), shift register #1147 (1148) and Build Array #2626 (4168).
- **#2765 is an input tunnel of a case structure.** Its outside terminal 2811 is a sink.
- **Its two inside terminals sit in different case frames, on separate wires:**
  - 2789 (frame 2235) → wire 3472 → #6085 (terminal 6253)
  - 2792 (frame 2265) → wire 2924 → #5696 (terminal 5910)
- **#5058's own `'x,y,z array'` input (5164) is on wire 5975**, which does not touch #2765 at all.

So "#2765 feeds one branched wire that reaches #5058, #5696 and #6085" has the direction backwards. It also merges three separate wires into one.

**The diff code rules out deletion as well.** `edges()` (`tools/stagexec.py:165-178`) skips any terminal whose `wire_uid` is 0. A terminal whose wire LabVIEW deleted could therefore never appear in `dangling_real_only`. All four terminals do appear there. So they still sit on wires that exist, and each of those wires is missing either its source or its sink.

The outside of the tree was not deleted either. The sinks on wire 505 (2811, 1148, 4168) do not appear in the diff, so real and simulated agree on them.

## 2. Alternative explanation: the tunnel lost its direction

Once #5058 moves, wire 505 has no source. #2765 then has no source on either side. NI documents that a tunnel with no source "cannot decide the direction of dataflow" and becomes an undirected tunnel ([NI: Wire connected to an undirected tunnel](https://www.ni.com/docs/en-US/bundle/labview-api-ref/page/errors/wire-connected-to-undirected-tunnel.html)).

Its inside terminals 2789 and 2792 then stop reading as sources. Wires 3472 and 2924 become sink-only. That produces exactly what the log shows: both edges gone and all four terminals dangling.

The simulator already has a rule for this effect, but a narrower one. `_flip_orphaned_output_tunnels` (`tools/stagesim.py:308-324`) flips direction only when:

- the tunnel is an **output** tunnel (its inside end is a sink on a wire with no source), and
- the tunnel class is `LoopTunnel` or `Tunnel` (`TUN1`, line 58).

Our case is the mirror image: an **input** `SelectorTunnel`. Neither the direction nor the class is covered. The rule's own evidence comes from cycle 71 (terminals 2043 and 5050 flipped `True->False`), which is the same phenomenon on a different tunnel class.

A side point: the brief says "the final end state agrees". That holds only at the `computation_diff` level. In `step_27_wire.json`, wires 3472 and 2924 are still fully wired. The simulator never removes them. #5696 and #6085 count as open in the end state only because their upstream chain dies at wire 505, which has no source.

## 3. What would falsify the claim

- **Claim holds if** a real terminal dump after op 3 shows `wire_uid 0` on 2789, 2792, 5910 and 6253 (wires deleted). The `edges()` logic above already makes this nearly impossible.
- **Tunnel-flip explanation holds if** the dump shows wires 3472 and 2924 still present, with `is_source` False on 2789 and 2792.
- **A third possibility** (LabVIEW renumbered the wires) would show non-zero but different wire uids on the two ends of each pair.

## 4. Cheapest discriminating test

The real graph after op 3 was not saved. `stage_d1_k.json:598-643` holds only the diff. So one headless read is needed, with no build and no save:

1. Replay ops 1–3 on a scratch copy.
2. Dump `term_uid`, `is_source` and `wire_uid` for terminals 2789, 2792, 5910, 6253 and 2811.
3. Optionally read `Wire.Is Broken?` on wires 3472 and 2924. The tunnel-flip explanation predicts they read as broken.

**If the flip is confirmed, the fix is:**

- Widen the simulator's tunnel-direction rule to every tunnel class (`SelectorTunnel` included) and to both directions: a tunnel with no source on either side reads all of its terminals as `is_source=False`.
- Then re-simulate and re-finalize, and keep E1 as it is.

**What would change my mind:** a dump showing `wire_uid 0` on those four terminals, or wire 505's sinks missing in the real graph.
