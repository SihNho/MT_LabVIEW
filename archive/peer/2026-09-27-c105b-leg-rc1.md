## Answer

**Verdict: the claim holds. Nothing that the diagnostic predicted would pass failed in this log.** Two corrections to how it is framed follow, and one hazard the log hides.

**Why rc=1 appears, from the machine's record**
- `tools/bgrun.py:297` only collects lines that *start* with `RESULT {`. The motor-gate RESULT lines are indented under `[   6.3s]     | `, so they are never collected. `tools/bgrun.py:325-331` sets rc=1 when **any** collected RESULT line failed, and the END line quotes the first failing one (`bad[0]`).
- Only two RESULT lines were collected:
  - line 948: the reused inner driver `drive_m8`, FAIL 4/4 on gates M2–M5.
  - line 960: the diagnostic's own verdict, PASS 10/0.
- The script then called `os._exit(0)` (`diag_c105b_leg.py:212`). That matches "the process itself said 0".
- So the claim says "the first RESULT line"; strictly it is the first *failing* one. Here the two coincide.

**1. Strongest reason the claim is wrong, or at least incomplete**
- The claim leaves out a fifth failure in the same inner block: `=== D0 v5: 12 pass, 1 fail === FAILING: 30 run2 RESTART LEG` (log:938-939, STEP 30 at log:492: `NOT started: ExecState=-1`).
- Step 30 did **not** fail by construction. It failed because the stop failed. `stop_with_fallback` returned `[false, "COM Abort RAISED", 112 s, "apartment2 abort did not return in 20s"]` (log:489), and after that `ExecState` could not be read (-1).
- Look at `drive_original_copy_v5.py:560-565`. If the Abort had returned cleanly and the state read 0 or 1, v5 would have called `leg("run2")`. That is `probe_leg` again, because `diag_c105b_leg.py:191` replaces `v5.leg` with it. The result would have been:
  - a **second Run of the VI**, which opens VISA/COM again;
  - `NLEG=2`, so gate **C4a "one leg, no picks" would have FAILED**.
- So C4a passed only because the stop failed. The diagnostic's contract holds by accident, not by design.
- The same thing happened in 105-1: stop hung (`diag_c105_leg.log:474`), step 30 failed the same way (`:478`).

**2. Alternative explanation of the same evidence**
- "rc=1 is only the by-design M2–M5 failures" and "rc=1 also covers a hung Abort and a forced kill" produce the same END line.
- `bgrun` reports only `bad[0]`. If the outer diagnostic had also failed, the END line would still name M2.
- The END line cannot tell these apart. Only line 960 can, and it says PASS.
- Other failures happened but no gate covered them, so they are not failed predictions:
  - 38 panel reads timed out, cut off at `z step (um)`, taking 152 s (log:887-892).
  - A forced kill was needed (STEP 92, log:639: `forced kill used=True`, allowed by the docstring).
  - LabVIEW's handle count reached 65,700 (log:894), against about 34,000 at the start (log:10) and the ~31,500 baseline.
  - `Configure.vi` still reported `ExecState` 3 after the Abort (log:487). 3 means Running, a state that LabVIEW merges with "reserved": [LabVIEW Wiki](https://labviewwiki.org/wiki/VI_class/Execution.State_property), [NI forum](https://forums.ni.com/t5/LabVIEW/Why-is-execution-state-RUNNING/td-p/115411).
  - All of these match 105-1 (`diag_c105_leg.log:873-880`).

**3. What would falsify the claim**
Any one of these would:
- a gate at log:950-959 reading FAIL;
- `facts_c105b_dialog.json`'s gates containing a false value;
- a third collected RESULT line with FAIL status;
- a D0 step failing other than 30.

None is present.

**4. Cheapest test that separates the two readings**
This needs no LabVIEW, just two reads:
- `grep '^RESULT {' tools/bench/diag_c105b_leg.log` should give exactly two lines, 948 FAIL and 960 PASS.
- Check that `tools/bench/facts_c105b_dialog.json` `gates` are all true.

Then fix the hazard: have `probe_leg` force the run2 branch off (for example set `v5.RUN2_BUDGET_S = 0`), so C4a no longer depends on the Abort hanging.

**What would change my mind:** evidence that STEP 30 or the forced kill was listed as a predicted pass in the card's contract (`task_105-2.json`). The docstring says "forced kill allowed" and does not gate the stop succeeding.
