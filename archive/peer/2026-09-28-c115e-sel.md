## Answer

**Verdict: the rule-1a acceptance holds, but the claim reaches it through a step that measured nothing.** The sentence "every new loose-ends item can only sit on one of the 7 shared nets" is an inference: no file checked shows it, and the tool it rests on cannot see loose segments. Rule 1a is supported by a different measurement, and the loose-ends count licence planned on top of the claim is a real hole.

**1. The strongest reason the claim is wrong: the location argument is circular.**
- "No other net's terminal list changed" (`diag_c115d_graph.log:30`) cannot limit where a loose segment appears. The card itself says a free segment is not in `Wire.Terms[]` (`result_115-4.json:22`).
- The instrument is blind to exactly the thing being located. That the counts match (7 shared nets, 7 new items) is an arithmetic coincidence, not a measurement.
- What does support rule 1a is a separate fact: the full diff of terminal lists across all 1945 wires shows no wire added, 11 stubs gone, and on the 7 shared nets only one register sink terminal lost each, with the same source and live sinks (`diag_c115d_graph.log:17-30`).
- A segment with no terminal feeds no node, wherever it sits. So rule 1a does not depend on the location sentence, and that sentence should be struck from PD230(d).

**Q1. Can a net with an unchanged source and sinks still change what a sink receives? I found no concrete case in these files.**
- The 12 lost ids are 6 left and 6 right shift registers. Every left register's inner output was a stub with no sink: w1681, w6041, w7429, w9097, w10763, w28039 (`diag_c115d_graph.log:13-26`).
- No right register has a source row, so no final value left the loop.
- So the retired registers delivered nothing to any kept node. The initial values on w8590, w9051, w11253, w25438, w25461 and w29122 only fed dead registers.
- Coercion and type are decided by the source and the sink terminals, and those are unchanged.
- Tunnel indexing mode was not compared. However, the "undirected tunnel" items are 3 in B3 and 3 in R1 (B3/R1 captures, lines 249, 419, 460), and no tunnel's terminal list changed.
- One apparent class shift is not real. The m1 script counts B3 as not-connected 19 / other 11 and R1 as 20 / 10, but B3 capture line 1513 is a misread "anvthing". Both captures actually hold 20 "not connected to anything" items. Nobody needs to chase this.

**Q2. Could any new item sit on a net other than the 7? Yes, and nothing measured rules it out.**
- The same counts fit a swap: one shared net already had a loose end in B3 (so it adds no new item) while some unrelated net's segment became loose (so it adds one). The totals come out identical: 23 − 6 + 7 = 24.
- The bounding boxes cannot decide this. Each Show-Error shot is scrolled to its own item, so coordinates cannot be compared across items.
  - R1#14 is a thin vertical strip (x 18–62, y 3–651, `diag_c115d_sel.log:28`). That fits a vertical wire run near a border, but it names no net.
  - R1#13 and R1#19 have no mask at all (`:27,30`).
- Matching shots by eye is fragile here. Pixel-difference pairing (m1) found 4 pairs, where selection-mask pairing (sel) found 17 (`diag_c115d_m1.log:8-11` against `diag_c115d_sel.log:50-72`).
- Remove Bad Wires is no help either. Both runs end at 1905 wires, and none of the shared nets was deleted as a whole wire (`stage_d1_l2r1.log:241-245`). That tells us nothing about where the loose segments are.

**Q3. Is licensing 7 items by count, with no uid, a hole? Yes.**
- PD230(f) (`docs/d1-loop12-17-split-plan.md:2077`) carries a count licence forward into L2-R2. A later stage could then hide a new loose-ends item on an unrelated net under it.
- The cheapest bound that keeps it honest:
  - (a) Pin R1's loose-ends count at exactly 24, with no "up to".
  - (b) In every later stage, let the loose-ends count grow by no more than the number of nets that lost a sink in that stage's own full terminal-list diff.
  - (c) Make the whole-graph check (no other net's terminal list changed; source and live sinks equal) a gate in every stage, not an M5 measurement taken once.
  - (d) A count licence never outlives the stage that earned it.

**Alternative explanation of the same evidence.** The 7 new items are 6 register-side branches plus 1 segment on an unrelated net, with the seventh shared net already loose in B3. The counts cannot tell this apart from the claim.

**What would falsify the claim's location sentence.** After loose ends are removed from the 7 shared nets only, the count stays above 17. In other words, some loose item survives while every one of the 7 shared nets is clean.

**The cheapest test that separates the two.** Take a scratch byte copy of R1. Call `Wire.Remove Loose Ends` once on each of the 7 shared net uids, then read the Error List once.
- The claim predicts 17 loose-ends items (= 23 − 6).
- The alternative predicts 18 or more.
- This involves a delete on a copy, so it is a separate card. It is not needed for the rule-1a acceptance. It is needed before a licence of the form "dangling branches on the 7 nets" gets written into the expected file.

What would change my mind on rule 1a: any tunnel indexing mode, constant or node property that differs between B3 and R1. The M2 read compared terminal lists only, so this was not measured.
