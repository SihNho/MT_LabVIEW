## Answer

**Verdict: the core claim holds, but it is framed wrong in two places.** Neither dry stop tells us anything about LabVIEW. Both are problems on our side, and both were caught offline. But dry 1 is not a plan-authoring bug. And the proposed checkpoint fix treats the symptom, not the class of fault.

## 1. Strongest reason the claim is wrong: dry 1 is a gap between the simulator and the executor, not a plan bug

- **The plan was legal.** stagesim finalized it (`final=True`, `open_rows_match`), and it addresses rows by uid. The executor then refused 5 of those rows because a LoopTunnel is not in `Diagram.Nodes[]` (`stagexec.py:634`) and has no owner route (`OWNER_ROUTED = ("SelectorTunnel","Tunnel")`, `stagexec.py:614`). So "finalized" does not mean "the executor can address it". That gap is in the tools, not in the plan.
- **This is the fourth time the same error has appeared**, each time for a new object class that nobody had enumerated:
  - SelectorTunnel and Tunnel: `stage_d1_l2a1_r2.log:37` ("node #10465 / #5603 not in Diagram[48] (#639).Nodes[]")
  - ControlTerminal: `stage_d1_l2a1_r3.log:439`
  - constants: PD188(c), `stagexec.py:652-656`
  - LoopTunnel: now
- **Calling it "authoring" leads to the wrong fix.** Moving the rows to B2 works around the gap. The underlying fault stays: stagesim's finalize does not run the executor's addressability rule. That fault will come back for the next class nobody has listed.

## 2. Alternative explanations

**Dry 1:** as above, the executor's address model is incomplete, not the plan.

- The attack on the rows kept in B1 mostly fails, and I checked it. Every tunnel end still in B1 belongs to CaseStructure #2222, which moves at op 3: #2276 is a `Tunnel`, and #6132, #2451 and #7091 are `SelectorTunnel`s (`graph_l2a3_bed_20260927.json`). Those classes are owner-routed.
- There is real-run precedent for exactly this: L2-A1 moved case structures #5540 and #10445 (`sim/l2a1/stageplan_l2a1.json:15,51`), wired their faces afterwards, and passed E1 over 42 ops (`stage_d1_l2a1_86-5.log:660`).
- The dropped tunnels do sit on moved or other loops. #30135 and #30896 are at x=4819, the position of ForLoop #29874, which moves at op 7. #10004, #31051 and #31137 are at x=5102. So the brief is right to push them to B2 together with a measured LoopTunnel route.
- For that route: LoopTunnel sits under Tunnel in the VI Server class hierarchy ([Tunnel class](https://labviewwiki.org/wiki/Tunnel_class), [LoopTunnel class](https://labviewwiki.org/wiki/LoopTunnel_class/Condition_Terminal_property)). The `Addr.tun` route (Tunnel.Outside Terminal, `stagexec.py:974-991`) is therefore the likely candidate. I could not confirm on the web that LoopTunnel inherits `Outside Terminal`; that has to be measured.

**Dry 2:** it is a recipe bug, but the fix is aimed at the symptom.

- The executor already knows exactly which checkpoints it needs (`stagexec.py:1322`). Yet every recipe re-derives its own set by hand, and this recipe copied a rule from L2-A1 that never met two `add_shift_reg` ops in a row.
- Fix J3 moves the knowledge of `BIND_KINDS` into the recipe again, so the next recipe can repeat the mistake. The fix for the whole class is for `Executor` to take the union of the required set and the recipe's set itself, instead of refusing.
- There is also a unit mismatch. The recipe's existing rule counts **plan actions** (`stage_d1_l2b1.py:18-19`), while the executor counts **real ops** (`enumerate(self.ops,1)`). The two diverge as soon as a `tunnel` group turns 3 actions into 1 op (`compile_plan`, `stagexec.py:390-400`). Today only the L1 gate (`len(OPS)==len(A)`, fatal) prevents that. The J3 formula `compile_plan(plan)[k-1]` counts ops, so it will be mixed with an action-counted set.

**Gaps the J1 re-plan opens, which your hypothesis does not cover:**

1. **The dry run cannot check the new moves.** `DryPlanBE.move_in` checks only the destination diagram (`stagexec.py:2060-2063`), not whether the moved object can be found. The real move op (`build_d1_v0.move_in`, `tools/recipes/build_d1_v0.py:318`) is copied from `probe_move_ctlterm_v0`, so it was proven on ControlTerminals. I found no evidence it has ever moved a `*Constant` in a real run. A dry PASS will therefore say nothing about moving #6404, #9050 and #9906.
2. **A wire across a structure border creates an object that is never checkpointed.** In LabVIEW, a wire across a border makes a tunnel, but it compiles as a `connect` op, which is not in `BIND_KINDS`. None of the 6 current B1 wires cross a border: after the moves, every end is on #23166, including #5058 (`frame_diagram 23166`). Each of the 4 wires J1 adds is safe only if **both** of its ends are among the moved objects.

## 3. What would falsify the claim

Either of these, in dry 3 or the real run:

- a stop, `STEP-DIFF`, or extra object at any op that touches a face of #2222 after op 3, or a constant move;
- a checkpoint diff showing an object nobody planned right after a `connect` op.

## 4. Cheapest test that separates the two readings (offline, no LabVIEW)

1. On the re-finalized plan, for every `wire` action, compare the `frame_diagram` of its source and its sink in the sim state just before that step. If any pair differs, that wire creates a tunnel implicitly, and the `BIND_KINDS` union leaves a gap.

If both come back clean (all pairs on the same diagram, and a constant move already recorded), your hypothesis holds with nothing left out.
