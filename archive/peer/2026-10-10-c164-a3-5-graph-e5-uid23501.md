## Answer

**Verdict: I tried to refute the claim and could not. It holds.** Uid 23501 was freed when the tunnel was deleted at op 4, and LabVIEW handed it to terminal `y` of the new Equal? at op 7. E5 checks by uid alone, which is unsafe, so it fails on a correct file. One thing to correct: comparing uid plus class works here only by luck. The safe rule is that no `LoopTunnel` at the deleted node's owner and position survives.

## 1. Strongest reason the claim could be wrong, and why it fails
The best case against it would be that the tunnel survived under another uid, or that the op-9 connect landed on something other than Equal?.y.
- **The tunnel did not survive.** The base graph's LoopTunnel list is identical to the adopted graph's list except that 23501 is missing. That loop edge had six tunnels at x=15675…15725. The adopted graph has five (15685…15725), and the one at 15675 is gone. No new LoopTunnel uid exists anywhere, which matches CENSUS-ALL `LoopTunnel: -1` (`diag_c164_p5s02d_scratch.log:222`).
- **The connect landed on the right terminal.** The op-9 readback is `{'UID': 12170, 'Name': 'y', 'Is Broken?': False}` (scratch log :163), meaning sink = Comparison #12170, term `y`. The label `#23474->#23501` is just the terminal uids of that wire's two ends. In the adopted graph, wire 31744 has exactly two rows:
  - source 23474 `records final (1.2 to 1.7)`, owner Local #12169;
  - sink 23501 `y`, owner Comparison #12170.
- **It matches the plan.** `plan_ring_p5s02d.json:224-226` has `w_eq7_y: src new:LR7.value → dst new:EQ7.y`, executed as op 9 (`plan_ring_p5s02d_pred.json:217-222`). So the w_eq7_y connect is exactly what the plan intended.

## 2. Alternative explanation, and why it fails too
The uid might be a number made up by our reader, not LabVIEW's own object id. Then "re-use" would be an artefact of our tooling. This is refuted:
- The same uids (12170, 23501, 23481) appear in the live build log and in a graph read hours later, in a fresh LabVIEW, from the saved file. So they are stored in the file.
- NI's own documentation says a deleted object's UID may later be given to a different object, and that you should check Class Name along with the UID ([GObject UID property](https://labviewwiki.org/wiki/GObject_class/UID_property)).
- The project has hit this before: UID-REUSE #23276 (a deleted Comparison's terminal reappeared as a new Local's sink) at `docs/d1/ring-p4b.md:192-198`, also `docs/d1-loop12-17-split-plan.md:921-922` and `docs/d1/INDEX.md:313`.

## 3. A second re-use in the same run, which matters for the fix
**Uid 23481 was re-used too.** In the base graph it is terminal `x` of the deleted Comparison #23042. In the adopted graph it is terminal `x = y?` of the new Comparison #12170. Both are `ParameterTerminal`.
- So "uid + class" would also have passed a *wrong* file in this case: a surviving #23042 terminal would have had the same class.
- The safe check for a deleted node is: no object of the deleted class sits at the deleted node's owner and position. Matching uid + class + owner uid also works.

## 4. What would falsify the claim
Any one of these, in `graph_ring_p5s02d_20261010_154830.json`:
- a `LoopTunnel` owned by While #23041 at or near (15675, 11867), under any uid;
- any LoopTunnel uid that is not in the base graph;
- wire 31744's sink not being (owner 12170, `y`);
- the old tunnel's terminals 23505 or 23514 still present.

From my greps: none of these appear (23505 and 23514 are listed as raw_lost at scratch log :225). So the claim stands.

## 5. Cheapest discriminating test
It takes two JSON loads, no LabVIEW:
1. Compare the sets `{uid for objs if class=="LoopTunnel"}` between the base and adopted graphs. The result should be base − adopted = {23501} and adopted − base = ∅. I confirmed this by grep.
2. For uid 23501, compare (class, owner, pos) between the two files: base `(LoopTunnel, WhileLoop, [15675,11867])`, adopted `(ParameterTerminal, Comparison #12170, [5478,11923])`.
3. Rewrite E5's deleted-node check as `cls.get(u) == base_cls[u] and owner(u) == base_owner[u]`, and re-run it against the stored graph JSON.

## Separate finding: the scratch's own re-use detectors missed both re-uses
The scratch run reported `reuse_noted: []` (log :220) and `recycled_uids: []` (log :225), even though 23501 (a node uid that came back as a terminal) and 23481 (a terminal moved to another owner) were both re-used within that run.
- Those detectors are blind to at least these two cases. That is a defect in our tooling, not in the VI.
- E5 was copied from `diag_c163_a2_2_graph.py` without the uid-re-use rule the project had already recorded. Because this re-use problem has come up before, it counts as a repeated failure, not a one-off.

**What would change my mind:** a LoopTunnel row at the old position under a new uid in a re-read of the graph, or a later LabVIEW Error List entry pointing at a dangling tunnel on While #23041.
